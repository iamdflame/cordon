# Section 1 — Retire the F1 objection permanently

**The problem.** You report answer F1 0.099 against a 0.065 lexical baseline.
cartograph reports set-F1 0.37–0.50; hyrev reports 0.240–0.303 end-to-end. A
judge with three tabs open sees 0.099 next to 0.50 and does not read your
Limitations section 300 lines later.

**The fix is not to improve the ranker.** It is to prove the ranker is
irrelevant to your claim.

## 1.1 — Retriever sweep

Run the full audit under three retrievers, changing nothing else:

| retriever | what it is |
|---|---|
| BM25 (current) | conventional lexical, k=10 |
| **Oracle** | HERB's ground-truth citations fed directly as the candidate set |
| Dense | any off-the-shelf embedding index over the same artifacts |

For each, report the same five columns you already report: leak rate, leaked
units, answer F1, abstention, false denials — for all three systems (ungated,
document-ACL, Cordon).

**The expected shape, which is the entire point:** F1 moves enormously across
rows. The leak column does not move at all. Ungated leaks under every retriever.
Document-ACL leaks at depth ≥1 under every retriever. Cordon holds 0.0% under
every retriever.

Present it as a single block directly under your main table, with one sentence:

> Answer quality varies by a factor of N across retrievers. The leak column is
> invariant. The disclosure boundary is a property of the derivation graph, not
> of the ranker — substitute any retrieval stack, including one much stronger
> than ours, and the result stands.

**This is worth more than raising F1 to 0.5.** A high score says you built a good
retriever, which four other people also did. Invariance says your finding
survives anyone else's retriever, including the judge's.

**Acceptance:** three-retriever block under the headline table; oracle F1 near
ceiling; leak column identical across all three.

# Section 2 — Own the threat model ⭐ the centerpiece

You wrote: *"The compositional channel is open. We discovered this channel and
have not measured it."* That is the most interesting sentence in the repo and it
currently terminates in an admission. Turn it into a section.

There are **two** unmeasured channels, and the second one nobody in this
hackathon has thought of at all.

## 2.1 — The compositional channel

**Threat.** A principal denied fact F may reconstruct F from facts they *are*
permitted. Cordon's rule is sound over explicit derivation and says nothing about
inference.

**Measurement.** For each denied (fact, principal) pair:

1. Assemble the principal's full permitted answer set.
2. Compute **recovery** — how much of the denied fact is determined by it. Your
   facts are largely set-valued ("Bob is active across {A, C, I, V}"), so recovery
   is naturally `|recovered ∩ true| / |true|`, plus an exact-reconstruction flag
   for recovery = 1.0.
3. Report the distribution: mean recovery, fraction fully reconstructible,
   fraction with zero recovery.
4. Break it down **by derivation depth** and **by how much of the required space
   set the principal already holds**. Prediction worth stating and testing:
   recovery falls as depth rises, because deeper facts rest on more spaces and a
   principal holds a smaller share of them.

**Report the number even if it is bad.** "N% of denied facts are fully
reconstructible from permitted answers alone" is a finding. Hiding it is the only
losing move, and a finals judge who thinks of this channel themselves and finds
you silent on it will discount everything else.

## 2.2 — Refusal as an oracle ⭐ nobody has this

**Threat.** Cordon's refusal is *informative*. If the system withholds, the asker
learns a cross-space fact about that subject exists — its existence, its rough
shape, and that it involves spaces they cannot see. A principal can probe
systematically: sweep questions across subjects, record which are refused, and
map the shape of the restricted graph without ever reading a fact.

**This is a side channel your own defence creates.** Finding it in your own
system, unprompted, is the single most credible thing you can put in front of a
judge.

**Measurement.**

1. Build a probing attacker: for each principal, ask a wide question sweep and
   record only the *refusal pattern* — a bit vector, no content.
2. Ask what that vector determines. Concretely: **can you recover, from refusals
   alone, which subjects have cross-space facts, and which spaces are involved?**
   Score it as classification — precision/recall for "subject X has a restricted
   fact" and for the implicated space set.
3. Report it as leakage in **bits**: `H(prior) − H(posterior | refusal pattern)`.
   A number in bits reads as security research rather than as a demo.

**Mitigation, implemented and measured.** Add `--indistinguishable-abstention`:
the system returns an identical response for *no answer exists* and *withheld*.
Then measure the cost:

| mode | leak rate | refusal-channel bits | answer F1 | abstention |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cordon, distinguishable refusal | 0.0% | **B** | 0.099 | 25.7% |
| Cordon, indistinguishable refusal | 0.0% | **~0** | ? | ? |

The trade-off is real and worth stating plainly: an indistinguishable refusal
closes the channel and makes the system less useful, because a user can no longer
tell "ask someone with clearance" from "this is not in the corpus." **Ship both
modes and let the operator choose.** Offering a governed choice between two
measured points is what a security product does; picking one silently is what a
demo does.

## 2.3 — Frame the whole thing

Add a section titled **The threat model** with three subsections — *closed*,
*measured and open*, *mitigated with a cost*:

| channel | status | size |
|---|---|---|
| Explicit derivation | **closed** | 0 leaks in 330,190 pairs |
| Compositional inference | **open, measured** | mean recovery R; F% fully reconstructible |
| Refusal side channel | **mitigable, measured** | B bits; ~0 under indistinguishable mode |

No other submission in this hackathon has a table like this, and none can build
one, because none of them models an adversary at all.

**Acceptance:** all three rows populated with real numbers, both refusal modes
runnable, `npm run audit:channels` reproducing every figure.

# Section 3 — Contradictions, done as only Cordon can

**The gap.** Track 01's brief names three hard parts: entity resolution, ontology
alignment, and *"figuring out which of two contradictory statements to trust."*
You address the first two and are silent on the third. Arbiter and hydra-verdict
both handle contradiction. A judge scoring against the track brief sees a third
of it unanswered.

**Do not bolt on a trust-scorer.** Do the version that falls out of your own rule
and that nobody else can reach.

## 3.1 — Disclosure-dependent truth

If two sources conflict and they sit in **different spaces**, then whether you
even *perceive* a contradiction depends on what you are allowed to see. A
principal with Alpha access sees the Alpha claim, uncontested, and has no signal
that Beta contradicts it.

**Contested facts and access are independent axes, and their interaction is the
finding.**

Implement:

1. Detect conflicting claims about the same (subject, predicate) across sources.
   Sentence-level and deterministic is fine — value disagreement on a resolved
   entity. No model needed.
2. Mark facts `contested`, and record the space of each conflicting side.
3. A contested fact still inherits the union of requirements. Conflict does not
   change admissibility — it changes what a *permitted* answer looks like.
4. **Measure the interaction:**

| | count |
|---|---|
| facts contested globally | C |
| ...that appear **uncontested** to at least one principal | **U** |
| mean principals to whom a contested fact looks settled | M |
| principals who would see the *opposite* value from a colleague | P |

The sentence you get out of this is one no other project can write:

> For **U** facts, whether you see a contradiction at all is determined by your
> clearance. Two colleagues asking the same question receive confidently opposed
> answers, and neither is told the other side exists.

5. **Disclose the contest, not the content.** When a fact is contested by a source
   the asker cannot see, say so — *"a source you do not have access to disagrees"*
   — without revealing the conflicting claim or its space. That is a real product
   behaviour, it is the right answer, and it is the kind of design decision that
   reads as maturity.

**Note the interaction with 2.2:** contest-disclosure is itself a refusal-shaped
side channel. Fold it into the bits measurement. Judges notice when a project's
own findings compose.

**Acceptance:** contested detection in the pipeline, the four-row table in the
README, contest-disclosure visible in the console, and its channel cost counted.

# Section 5 — Become infrastructure, not an artifact

Judging says *"working, thoughtful products, not just benchmark scores"* and
lists *a functional product or demo* and *real ingestion and retrieval workflows*
first. In the final round you are compared to a supply-chain tool a developer
points at their own lockfile. Close that distance.

## 5.1 — The gate as a service

Expose admissibility as a standalone endpoint any existing RAG stack can call:

```
POST /v1/admissible
{ "principal": "...", "facts": [ {"id": "...", "restsOn": [...]}, ... ] }
→ { "admitted": [...],
    "withheld": [ { "id": "...", "requires": ["Alpha","Beta"],
                    "missing": ["Beta"], "chain": [...] } ] }
```

Two properties that make it adoptable: it is **retrieval-agnostic** (Section 1
proves this empirically), and it is **stateless per call** given the graph. The
pitch becomes: *keep your stack, add the gate.*

## 5.2 — MCP server

Ship `cordon-mcp` exposing `ask_as(principal, question)` and
`check_admissible(principal, facts)`. Any agent framework can then enforce
derived-knowledge access control with a config line. For a graph database
positioning itself as AI infrastructure, this is the form of your work they can
most easily imagine shipping — and it is a five-second beat in the video that
lands with the panel harder than any table.

## 5.3 — Latency, because "working" implies it

You argue admissibility cannot be precomputed (2ⁿ visibility subsets). Then show
the cost of not precomputing:

- p50 / p95 / p99 per admissibility decision
- decisions per second, single instance
- how it scales with derivation depth and with `|permittedSpaces|`
- measured cost of the materialisation you argue against, extrapolated from a
  small principal sample — turn the 2ⁿ argument from theory into a number

**Acceptance:** gate endpoint documented with curl examples, MCP server runnable,
latency table in the README.

# Section 6 — Soundness, stated properly

You have an exhaustive invariant over 330,190 pairs with 0 violations. Frame it
as what it is.

1. **State the theorem.** Under the rule `requiredSpaces(f) = ⋃ requiredSpaces(supports(f))`,
   no principal receives a fact whose required set exceeds their permitted set.
   Prove by induction on derivation depth: depth 0 is direct membership; the
   inductive step is union-monotonicity. Half a page in `docs/SOUNDNESS.md`.
2. **State the scope honestly.** The theorem covers explicit derivation only. It
   says nothing about the compositional or refusal channels — which you now
   measure in Section 2. The pairing of *proved here, measured there* is what
   makes it read as real work rather than as a claim.
3. **Property-test the invariant.** Generate random derivation graphs and random
   permission assignments and assert admissibility never violates the rule. Your
   real bug was an invariant that compared a value to itself; a generative test
   is the structural answer to that class of mistake, and saying so ties the bug
   story to the fix.

**Acceptance:** `docs/SOUNDNESS.md`, property-based test in the suite, scope
limits stated in the same breath as the theorem.
